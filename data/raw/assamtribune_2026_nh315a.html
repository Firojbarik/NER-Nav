Rain-triggered landslides on NH-315A threaten Assam-Arunachal road link

Officials fear continued downpours could sever a key transport artery connecting communities across two states

By The Assam Tribune - 24 Jun 2026 3:27 PM IST| Updated On: 2026-06-24 10:02:46
X

Jorhat, June 24: Prolonged rainfall has triggered multiple landslides along National Highway 315(A), raising fears of a possible disruption in road connectivity between Assam and Arunachal Pradesh, on Wednesday.

Landslides have occurred at three locations along the highway linking Joypur in Assam's Dibrugarh district with Deomali and Khonsa in Arunachal Pradesh's Tirap district.

The affected stretch, which passes through Dehing Patkai National Park, was widened and reconstructed in recent years.

However, continuous rainfall over the past week has weakened hill slopes along the route, causing sections of the roadway to cave in towards deep gorges at three vulnerable points near the Kathalguri Beat Office under the Joypur Forest Division.


One of the affected spots is located near a notoriously hazardous U-turn along the highway.

As a result, nearly half of the road has collapsed at certain locations, leaving the route significantly narrowed and increasingly dangerous for commuters.

"It has been raining continuously for the last week, and landslides have occurred along the Joypur-Deomali highway. The situation is very risky. If the rains continue, there is a possibility that larger sections of the road may give way," a commuter said.

Although vehicular movement remains unaffected for now, the administration has imposed restrictions on traffic movement between 6 pm and 6 am as a precautionary measure.

The highway serves as a vital lifeline for residents of Khonsa, Deomali, Namsang and several other parts of Tirap district, who depend heavily on Assam for access to markets, healthcare facilities and other essential services.

Hundreds of commuters use the route daily to travel through Joypur and Naharkatia to Dibrugarh and Tinsukia.

While the National Highways Authority has installed safety barricades along the damaged stretches, concerns remain that continued erosion could completely sever road connectivity between Assam's Dibrugarh district and Arunachal Pradesh's Tirap district.

The development comes amid a broader weather alert across the region.

Earlier in the day, the Assam government placed the state on heightened alert following extremely heavy rainfall and flash floods in Arunachal Pradesh's Lower Subansiri district.

Authorities have warned of a likely rise in river levels across several downstream districts of Assam over the next 24 to 48 hours.

Teams of the State Disaster Response Force (SDRF), National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) and other emergency response agencies have been kept on standby for deployment should the situation deteriorate further.
